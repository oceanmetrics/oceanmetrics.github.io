# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two first-tier audiences, weighted equally. **Federal and agency contacts** — program managers and contracting officers' representatives at BOEM, NOAA, NASA, and NREL — arrive evaluating Ocean Metrics as a contractor or subcontractor for a specific program need. **Research program leads and principal investigators** — at institutions running programs like CalCOFI and the Marine Biodiversity Observation Network — arrive looking for a technical lead or collaborator who can build and sustain the analytical infrastructure their science depends on.

Both come with a real project in mind and limited patience for marketing. They are technically literate, skeptical of vague claims, and want to know quickly whether the person behind the studio has done work at their scale and standard. The site has to read as credible to a federal COR and to an academic PI without pandering to either.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio and marketing site for Ocean Metrics LLC — the solo practice of Ben Best, PhD (formerly EcoQuants LLC). It exists to establish technical credibility and generate qualified inquiries by showing the real analyses, interactive maps, and open-data platforms behind current and past ocean programs. Success is a visitor exploring the project work, coming away convinced the work is serious and reproducible, and reaching out about their own program.

## Positioning

The data-science studio behind federal and research ocean programs — the technical engine other institutions rely on to turn ocean observations into working, reproducible tools.

## Conversion & proof

- **Primary CTA:** explore the project work — portfolio-first, low-pressure. The live projects carry the argument. **Secondary CTA:** email to scope a project, for the visitor the work has already convinced.
- **The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds:** Ocean Metrics is the studio behind the models, maps, and platforms that federal and research ocean programs actually run on.
- **Belief ladder** (what the visitor must accept, in order, before reaching out):
  1. This is real, serious ocean data science — not consultancy boilerplate.
  2. Named agencies and programs already depend on this work (BOEM, NOAA, NASA, NREL; CalCOFI, MBON).
  3. The deliverables are reproducible and durable — open, callable, testable, and still working after the engagement ends.
  4. This studio could do the same for my program.
- **Proof on hand:** client/partner logos (BOEM, NOAA, NASA, NREL, NCEAS); the live project work itself (MarineSensitivity, CalCOFI, MarineBON) as primary evidence; peer-reviewed publications and cited work available to surface. No testimonials are claimed unless real quotes are supplied.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, reproducible, trustworthy. The voice is that of a working scientist, not a salesperson: precise, plain-spoken, and grounded in evidence. Credibility comes from showing the work and its provenance, never from adjectives. Confident without swagger; the restraint itself signals expertise. Warmth and curiosity are present but subordinate to rigor — the site should feel like it was made by someone who measures carefully and shows their work.

## Anti-references

Steer clear of all four common traps:
- **Generic consultancy template** — stock handshake photography, gradient hero, interchangeable "solutions" language, vague buzzwords.
- **Startup SaaS landing** — the hero big-number template, endless identical feature-card grids, "Trusted by" logo walls used as filler rather than evidence.
- **Dated academic homepage** — a wall of dense text, no hierarchy, a CV dump, unstyled links; credible but visually inert.
- **Over-designed / flashy** — animation for its own sake, effects that upstage the content and undercut the scientific seriousness.

The design has to thread between visually inert and visually overwrought: distinctive and considered, but always in service of the evidence.

## Design Principles

- **Let the work prove it.** Show real projects, real agencies, real outcomes as evidence. The portfolio does the persuading; copy stays out of the way.
- **Practice what you preach.** Reproducibility is the pitch, so the site itself must be clean, fast, open, and correct. Sloppy craft would contradict the message.
- **Credible to two rooms at once.** Every page has to hold up for a federal COR and an academic PI simultaneously — concrete and provenance-forward, never pitched down to either.
- **Provenance as proof.** Named institutions, named programs, and linkable live work carry more weight than testimonials or adjectives. Attribution is a design element.
- **Restraint with a point of view.** Avoid both the inert academic page and the flashy template. Seriousness can still be distinctive; the goal is quiet confidence that is unmistakably designed, not defaulted.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Sensible, well-executed defaults rather than a formal certification target: body text at 4.5:1 or better, large text at 3:1 or better, full keyboard operability, visible focus states, semantic markup, and a genuine `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation. Not audited against a specific WCAG level, but nothing on the site should fail AA on the basics.
