**Ocean Metrics LLC** is a one-person, U.S.-registered environmental data science studio
(St. Petersburg, Florida). I build the analyses, interactive maps, and open-data platforms that ocean
science programs depend on — for federal agencies like BOEM and NOAA, and for research programs like
CalCOFI and the Marine Biodiversity Observation Network.

My background is in **marine spatial ecology**. I earned my PhD at Duke University's Marine Geospatial
Ecology Lab, building cetacean distribution models and contributing to OBIS-SEAMAP; served as a senior
analyst on the **Ocean Health Index** at NCEAS; and lectured on GIS and environmental informatics at
the UCSB Bren School and at Duke. Across all of it, one principle holds: science should be
**reproducible** — callable, testable, and open to inspection.

## From EcoQuants to Ocean Metrics

Ocean Metrics is the continuation of **EcoQuants LLC**, which I founded in 2017. The EcoQuants name
came from the Zen *ensō* — the incomplete circle — with a hidden "Q": a reminder to model the world
quantitatively while embracing its uncertainty. Ocean Metrics carries that idea forward, sharpened to
what I actually do: **measure the ocean**, honestly and rigorously, and hand back tools that keep
working after the project ends.

## How I work

- **Analyze** — reproducible pipelines in R and Python: species distribution models, spatial
  statistics, and data engineering that others can rerun and trust.
- **Develop** — interactive maps, dashboards, and open-data platforms backed by APIs, spatial
  databases, and cloud tiling.
- **Train** — as a certified Software &amp; Data Carpentry instructor, helping teams become
  self-sufficient in reproducible research and spatial analysis.
